Little-o & Little-ω (strict bounds)

o(g): $\lim_{n\to\infty} f(n)/g(n)=0$ — strictly smaller (e.g. $n=o(n^2)$).
ω(g): $\lim_{n\to\infty} f(n)/g(n)=\infty$ — strictly larger.
$f=\Theta(g) \iff f=O(g)$ AND $f=\Omega(g)$.

When Master Theorem fails

Gap cases — $f(n)$ between $n^{\log_b a}$ and $n^{\log_b a+\epsilon}$, e.g. $T(n)=2T(n/2)+n\log n$. Use recursion-tree → $\Theta(n\log^2 n)$, or Akra–Bazzi for unequal splits.

Akra–Bazzi (mini)

For $T(n)=\sum a_i T(n/b_i)+g(n)$, solution involves finding $p$ such that $\sum a_i b_i^{-p}=1$, then $T(n)=\Theta(n^p(1+\int_1^n g(u)/u^{p+1}du))$.

Amortized methods (beyond aggregate)

Accounting method: charge each op a fixed amount, save credits for expensive ones.
Potential method: define $\Phi(D)$ on data-structure state; amortized = actual + $\Delta\Phi$.

Common pitfalls

• Big-O is NOT tight — $n=O(n^{100})$ is true but useless.
• $\log$ base doesn’t matter inside $\Theta$ (constant factor).
• $n!=\Theta(n^n e^{-n}\sqrt n)$ — Stirling.
• $\log(n!)=\Theta(n\log n)$.
