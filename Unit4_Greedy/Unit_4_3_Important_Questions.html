Drill 6 — Huffman for “CYBER CRIME”

Frequencies: C=2, R=2, E=2, space=1, Y=1, B=1, I=1, M=1 (total 11 chars).
Min-heap (ascending freq): sp:1, Y:1, B:1, I:1, M:1, C:2, R:2, E:2 Merge sp+Y → 2; queue: B:1,I:1,M:1,C:2,R:2,E:2,2 Merge B+I → 2; queue: M:1,C:2,R:2,E:2,2,2 Merge M+2(spY) → 3; queue: C:2,R:2,E:2,2(BI),3 Merge C+R → 4; queue: E:2,2(BI),3,4 Merge E+2(BI) → 4; queue: 3,4,4 Merge 3+4 → 7; queue: 4,7 Merge 4+7 → 11 (root) Depth×frequency sum ≈ 32–34 bits (vs 8·3=24 chars×3 = 33 bits with fixed-3-bit) Note: Huffman usually beats fixed-length by 20–30 %.

Drill 8 — Why does greedy fail on 0/1 Knapsack?

Items (v=60,w=10), (v=100,w=20), (v=120,w=30); W=50. Ratio-greedy picks items 1 and 2 → value 160, remaining capacity 20 useless because item 3 is too big. Optimal is items 2+3 → 220. Lesson: indivisibility breaks the exchange argument → need DP.
